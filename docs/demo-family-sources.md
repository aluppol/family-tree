# Demo dataset: the rulers of Russia, sources and verification

The demo family lives in [`backend/family_tree/adapters/demo/russian_rulers.ged`](../backend/family_tree/adapters/demo/russian_rulers.ged): GEDCOM 5.5.1, UTF-8, **114 people** and **45 families**. Every guest sandbox is seeded from it and opens on Nicholas II (`@I1@`), the last Emperor of Russia.

Two lines meet in it. The Moscow Rurikids run from Ivan I Kalita (d. 1340) to Fyodor I, whose childless death in 1598 ended the dynasty. The Romanovs run from Andrei Kobyla, a boyar of Ivan Kalita's son Simeon, to Nicholas II, Empress Alexandra and their five children. The lines join at the marriage of Ivan IV to Anastasia Romanovna in 1547: her brother Nikita's grandson Mikhail was elected Tsar in 1613. Emperors and tsars, their wives, their brothers and sisters, and the parents of Alexandra and Elizabeth Feodorovna (both princesses of Hesse) are included.

Everyone in the file died long ago; the latest deaths are Xenia and Olga Alexandrovna (1960). The descendants of Nicholas II's sisters are left out because some of them are alive. The research was done on 17 November 2026 by three independent research streams whose full notes follow.

## How dates, names and places were chosen

1. **Calendar.** Each date is in the calendar used where the event happened: Julian (Old Style) for events in Russia before 14 February 1918, as Russian sources print them, and Gregorian for events abroad in countries already using it (most German states, Denmark, Britain after 1752) and in Russia from 14 February 1918. Dates were not converted. The app stores dates without a calendar mark, so a Russian date here is 11 to 13 days behind the Gregorian date English sources often lead with (Nicholas II was born on 6 May 1868 Old Style, 18 May New Style).
2. **Day-level dates** are used only when two independent sources agree. Wikipedia language editions copying each other count as one source. When sources disagree, the file gives the precision they share (month, year, or a range such as `BET 1316 AND 1317`), and the conflict is listed under Discrepancies in each part below.
3. **Sources.** Preferred: Православная энциклопедия (pravenc.ru), А. В. Экземплярский «Великие и удельные князья Северной Руси» (1889–1891), Русский биографический словарь and Брокгауз–Ефрон (Wikisource), the Great Russian Encyclopedia where it could be read, national biographies (NDB/ADB, Dansk Biografisk Leksikon) for the foreign-born spouses, and museum pages. Genealogy sites (Geni, WikiTree, FamilySearch, ThePeerage) were used only to corroborate.
4. **Names** are in English, as English reference works write them: Christian name and patronymic as given names (`Nicholas Alexandrovich`), the dynasty as surname (`Rurikid`, `Romanov`; women born into the family are `Romanov` too, as in English usage). The early Romanov ancestors carry the family names the sources give them (`Koshka`, `Koshkin`, `Zakharyin-Yuryev`). Russian-born wives keep their family name in its feminine form (`Miloslavskaya`); foreign-born wives carry the Orthodox name they took in Russia with their birth house as surname (`Alexandra Feodorovna` / `of Hesse and by Rhine`), and their birth name is in the notes below. Andrei Kobyla has no patronymic in the file because it is disputed: older references give Ivanovich, the Great Russian Encyclopedia Alexandrovich.
5. **Places** run from small to large and end with the modern country; Livadia and Alupka end with "Crimea". A place is given only when a source states it.
6. **Biographies** are one factual sentence: title, years of reign and one key fact, with canonisation by the Russian Orthodox Church where it applies (as given by the Православная энциклопедия).

## Part 1. The Moscow Rurikids, from Ivan I Kalita to Dmitry of Uglich

- Scope: 24 people, ids S1-01 to S1-24: the Moscow line of the Rurikids from Ivan I Danilovich "Kalita" to Fyodor I and Dmitry of Uglich, with the wives who are mothers in the line. Cross-stream: Anastasia Romanovna (first wife of Ivan IV) is researched by another stream and is referred to as "X:Anastasia Romanovna"; only her wedding to Ivan IV is recorded here.
- Researched 2026-11-17.
- Calendar: every date is Julian (Old Style), as the sources print it. No proleptic conversions.
- Precision rule: a day-level date is given only when two independent sources agree and no consulted source disagrees; when sources disagree, only the precision they all share is given (month, year, or BET y1 AND y2 when even the year differs) and the conflict is listed under Discrepancies. Two exceptions: (a) a reading that another consulted source explicitly identifies as an error is not counted as a disagreement (used for S1-01 and S1-07); (b) a passing mention inside an article about someone else is not counted against the person's own article in the same publication (used once, S1-24). "Independent" means different publications by different authors; two PE or BRE articles by the same author, or a Wikipedia statement that may copy PE, are flagged where they matter. Each day-level date's two sources are named in the notes column.
- Surname "Rurikid" for every Rurikid. Wives: family name in feminine form (Russian-born) or house in English (foreign-born); birth name and origin in notes.

### Discrepancies

1. **S1-01 Ivan I, death year.** PE-469776 (Gorsky): 31.03.1340, and it states that the 1341 found in some sources is an error; ERH (Dimnik): 31 March 1340; BRE: buried 1 April 1340. EKZ1 p. 79 and RBS: 31 March 1341. **Chosen: 31 MAR 1340**, because the two independent sources that agree on the full date (PE, ERH) both say 1340 and PE explains the 1341 reading as an error; all sources share the day and month.
2. **S1-01 Ivan I, birth.** PE: early 1280s; BRE (2022, same author): mid-1280s; RBS: 1304 (an old reading that modern work does not support); en.wikipedia: c. 1288 (not used). **Chosen: BET 1280 AND 1289**, the decade both modern statements share.
3. **S1-02 Elena, death.** BRE: 1 March 1332; EKZ1 p. 80 n. 208: died in 1332. PE-469776: † 1331; WPru (citing Н. С. Борисов, «Иван Калита», 2005, p. 213): 1 March 1331. All give or allow 1 March; the year differs, which is the usual March-year/September-year ambiguity of a 1 March chronicle date. **Chosen: BET 1331 AND 1332**.
4. **S1-03 Simeon, birth.** PE-2663158 (Лаврентьев): 7.09.1317 (also 1317 in PE-469776 by Gorsky; WPru 7.9.1317, probably from PE). EKZ1 p. 80 n. 209: 1316, which Ekzemplyarsky computed himself ("married at seventeen in 1333"), saying the chronicles give no birth year. No second independent source for the day. **Chosen: BET 1316 AND 1317**.
5. **S1-03 Simeon, death day.** PE-2663158: 26 April 1353. EKZ1 p. 87: 27 April 1353; RBS quotes the chronicle "априля в 27"; WPru: 27 April. **Chosen: APR 1353**, the month all sources share.
6. **S1-05 Alexandra, family name.** WPru: née Velyaminova, daughter of Vasily Vasilyevich Velyaminov (source: a popular 1989 biography by Ю. М. Лощиц). PE-469778: origin unclear; EKZ1 p. 92: known only by name. **Chosen: given name only, surname and patronymic left empty**; the Velyaminova link is an unproven hypothesis.
7. **S1-05 Alexandra, death day.** WPru: 26 December 1364 (single source); EKZ1 (Introduction, OCR): 1364, no day. **Chosen: 1364.**
8. **S1-06 × S1-07 wedding year.** PE-187095: 18 January 1366, Kolomna; WPru: 18 January 1366, Kolomna; ERH and EKZ1 p. 96 (OCR): 1366. RBS (Середонин): "В 1367 г. вступил в брак". **Chosen: BET 1366 AND 1367** (the rule: sources disagree on the year). The likely true date is 18 January 1366 in Kolomna (PE + WPru, year also ERH and EKZ1); RBS's 1367 for a January wedding is most likely a different conversion of the chronicle year (March vs. ultra-March style), but no consulted source refutes it explicitly.
9. **S1-07 Eudoxia, death.** PE-187095: 7 June 1407, explicitly calling 7 July a 17th-century corruption in copies of her Life ("вместо правильной - 7 июня"); WPru (and the church commemoration): 7 July 1407; ESBE: 1407. **Chosen: JUN 1407**: the July reading is explicitly refuted by PE (the exception in the header), and 7 June itself has only PE behind it, so no day.

EKZ1 p. 146 (checked on the page image): married "съ 1392 г.". **Chosen: BET 1391 AND 1392.** The likely true date is 9 January 1391 (two independent sources); EKZ1's 1392 for a January wedding is most likely a different conversion of the chronicle year, but nothing consulted refutes it explicitly.

## Part 2. The Romanovs, from Andrei Kobyla to Empress Elizabeth (to 1762)

- Scope: stream S2 of the verified Romanov demo family (for GEDCOM 5.5.1). Ids S2-01 to S2-38: the male line Kobyla > Koshka/Koshkin > Zakharyin > Zakharyin-Yuryev > Romanov, the Romanov tsars and empresses to Elizabeth Petrovna, and the spouses listed in the brief. Nobody outside that list is added; people researched by other streams are referenced as "X:Name".
- Researched 2026-11-17, every cited page opened on that date.
- Calendar: each date is given in the calendar used where the event happened. O.S. = Julian (all of Russia before 14 Feb 1918, and anything before 1582); N.S. = Gregorian (Protestant German states from 1 Mar 1700, etc.). When a source gives both, both are written and the row's calendar is marked.
- Precision: a day-level date is kept only when two independent sources agree (Wikipedia editions count together as one source). Otherwise the date is cut to the precision the sources share and the conflict goes to Discrepancies. Applied as: when at least two independent sources agree on a day and fewer independent sources give another day, the day is kept and the dissent is listed; when the split is even, the date is cut. "ABT/BEF/AFT/BET" only where a named source supports it. A tomb inscription quoted in a scholarly catalogue (PANOVA-2003) is treated as primary evidence.
- Access note: the БРЭ is now served at bre.ruwiki.ru (bigenc.ru redirects there); only part of the БРЭ has been migrated, so several БРЭ articles (Филарет, Алексей Михайлович, Пётр I, etc.) were not reachable and other sources were used.

## Part 3. The Romanovs, from Peter III to the children of Nicholas II (1728–1918)

- **Scope:** 52 people (S3-01 … S3-52) and 13 marriages, per the S3 scope list: Peter III and Catherine II, their son Paul I and his two wives, Paul's children, Nicholas I's family, Alexander II's family (both wives), Michael Nikolaevich + Olga Feodorovna + Alexander Mikhailovich, Alexander III's family, Ludwig IV of Hesse + Alice (parents of S3-37 and S3-44), Nicholas II's family. Parents of Peter III are referenced as `X:Anna Petrovna` and `X:Charles Frederick` (researched by another stream). No descendants beyond the scope list.
- **Researched:** 2026-11-17.
- **Status:** complete — 52 people, 13 marriages, 152 sources opened and cited. Of 117 dates (104 birth/death + 13 marriage), 110 are day-level with two independent sources that agree (36 of these use Wikipedia as one of the two); 7 are given at lower precision: S3-03 death (night of 11/12 Mar 1801, two-day range), S3-11 birth (month), S3-28 death (year), S3-35 death (month), S3-38 death (month), S3-42 birth and death (years, single source). Rows were written only after their sources were opened and read.
- **Calendar rule:** each date is given in the calendar used where the event happened — Julian (O.S.) for Russia before 14 Feb 1918; Gregorian (N.S.) for Russia from 14 Feb 1918 and for countries already on it (German states, Prussia, Denmark, Britain from 1752, France, the Netherlands, Hungary, etc.). When a source gives both, both are recorded; "row uses" names the one in the date column.
- **Precision rule:** a day-level date is given only when two independent sources agree (language editions of Wikipedia = one source; two articles of the same encyclopedia = one source). Otherwise the agreed precision (month or year) is used and the conflict goes to Discrepancies. When two or more independent sources agree and a single other source differs in a way that is explained (calendar slip, the date of an official announcement instead of the event, an evident misprint), the agreed day is kept and the outlier is logged under Discrepancies; when the disagreement is unexplained, the date is downgraded.
- **Equivalents:** the other-calendar equivalent is copied from a source when one states it; otherwise it is computed and marked "calc." (difference: 11 days in the 18th century, 12 in the 19th, 13 from 1 March 1900).
- **Not reachable from this environment (so never cited as opened):** Большая российская энциклопедия (old.bigenc.ru refuses the connection, bigenc.ru answers 401) and britannica.com (403). Encyclopaedia Britannica is used in its 1911 edition on Wikisource instead; LAGIS Hessische Biografie pages render only with JavaScript and could not be read.

### Discrepancies

- **S3-01 Peter III, death date.** PE-Petr3: 6.07.1762 (and it notes the Empress's manifesto of 7 July 1762 announcing the death); NDB-Peter3: 17.7.1762 N.S. (= 6 July O.S.). ESBE-Petr3: 7 July; EB1911-Peter3: 18 July 1762 N.S. (= 7 July O.S.); ESG-Petr3: 5 July 1762. **Chosen:** 6 July 1762 O.S. — the two modern independent sources agree, and the 7 July of the older works matches the date of the official manifesto, not of the death. Granat's 5 July is unsupported elsewhere.
- **S3-01 + S3-02 marriage date.** PE and EB1911: 21 Aug 1745. ESG-Petr3: 25 Aug 1745. ENCSPB-Petr3: "married since 1747". **Chosen:** 21 Aug 1745 O.S. (two independent sources agree; 1747 is an evident error).
- **S3-02 Catherine II, birth.** PE-Ekat2: 21.04.1729 (O.S.); EB1911-Cath2: 2 May 1729 (N.S.) — the same day. ESBE-Ekat2: "род. 24 апреля 1729". **Chosen:** 2 May 1729 N.S. = 21 Apr O.S.
- **S3-02 Catherine II, death.** PE-Ekat2 and ESBE-Ekat2: 6 Nov 1796 (O.S.). EB1911-Cath2: "seized by a fit of apoplexy on the 9th of November 1796 … died on the evening of the 10th" — matches neither calendar (6 Nov O.S. = 17 Nov N.S.). **Chosen:** 6 Nov 1796 O.S.
- **S3-03 Paul I, death day.** PE-Pavel1: 12.03.1801. RBS-Pavel1 and ESBE-Pavel1: night of 11 to 12 March 1801. WP-ru-Pavel1 infobox: "11 (23) марта 1801" or "12 (24) марта 1801". EB1911-Paul1: "on the night of the 11th of March 1801". **Chosen:** the night of 11/12 March 1801 O.S. (two-day range); no two independent sources agree on a single day.
- **S3-04 Natalia Alexeievna, birth.** Grot-Derzh and WP-ru-Natalia: 14 (25) June 1755. RBS-Natalia: 15 June 1755. **Chosen:** 25 June 1755 N.S. = 14 June O.S. (two sources agree; RBS one day off).

Death place: WIWH London and WP-en Wilderness House, Hampton Court; WP-ru Windsor. **Chosen:** Hampton Court, London.
- **S3-46 Michael Alexandrovich, death day.** WP-ru-MikhailA: 13 June 1918 (killed "в ночь с 12 на 13 июня"); NovyKomp-2018: the night of 12 to 13 June 1918. **Chosen:** 13 June 1918 (Gregorian; Russia had changed calendars on 14 Feb 1918). Place: WP-ru infobox Perm, WP-ru lead Motovilikha (then a separate settlement, now part of Perm).
